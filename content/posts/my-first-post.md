In recent months, there has been a lot of talk about AI assisted research, AutoResearch and the path to RSI. OpenAI is pursuing an automated AI researcher by March 2028, reporting strong progress toward that goal and claiming to have already achieved an automated research intern capable of carrying out well-defined research tasks under human direction.[^openai-research] There are a lot of neolabs starting with their core thesis revolving around RSI and autonomous research, including Jeff Dean's [Discovery Loop](https://www.discoveryloop.com/), [Recursive](https://www.recursive.com/) and [CoreAutomation](https://www.coreauto.com/), to name a few. CoreAuto's short-term approach seems to be amplifying human researchers through heavy use of AI, with humans still seeding ideas but much of the iteration loop automated away. Cofounder Jerry Tworek has even suggested that humans have roughly two more years of meaningful contributions to make to AI research.[^tworek-mts]

I myself have used AI assisted research heavily this year to land multiple records in the [modded-nanogpt](https://github.com/kellerjordan/modded-nanogpt) speedrun, both on the model architecture and systems side, introduce looped transformers to OpenAI's parameter golf, even being featured in [their official blog](https://openai.com/index/what-parameter-golf-taught-us/), running pretty much all the experiments for my independent research and most recently trying a whole lot of wild architectures for the [One Layer Deeper](https://onelayerdeeper.ai/problem) competition (a blog about everything I tried here is coming soon too).
